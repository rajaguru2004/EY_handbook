## 1. Why This Topic Matters for EY

**Weightage:** Blood relations 8–10% · Direction sense 7–9% of the reasoning section. Together they are worth **1–2 questions**.

**Why they are grouped:** both are solved the same way — **by drawing**. Neither topic has formulas worth memorising; both have a *notation* worth mastering. A candidate who draws consistently gets these right in 30 seconds. A candidate who tries to hold three generations or five turns in their head gets them wrong.

**The single most valuable habit in this topic: pick up the pen.** Rough paper is permitted in EY tests. Use it.

**Question styles reported:**

| Sub-topic | Typical shape |
|---|---|
| **Blood relations** | "Pointing to a man, a woman said, 'His mother is the only daughter of my mother'" |
| | "A is B's father, B is C's father — how is A related to C?" |
| | Coded relations: "A + B means A is the father of B" |
| | Family puzzles with professions and multiple couples |
| **Direction sense** | "Walks 5 km north, turns right, walks 3 km — where is he?" |
| | Shortest distance from the start (Pythagoras) |
| | Turning through angles (45°, 135°, 180°) |
| | Shadow-direction questions |

## 2. Core Concepts — PART A: BLOOD RELATIONS

### 2.1 The family-tree notation

Use one consistent set of symbols. This is the notation used throughout this handbook:

```
        ○  = female              □  = male              △ = gender unknown

        A ═══ B          horizontal double line  =  MARRIED couple
        │
        │                vertical line  =  PARENT above, CHILD below
        C

        D ─── E          horizontal single line  =  SIBLINGS
```

**A worked tree:**

```
      F ═══ E                    F and E are a married couple
         │
      ┌──┴──┐
      B     G                    B and G are their children
      │
      D                          D is B's child

  ⇒  F and E are D's grandparents
  ⇒  G is D's uncle or aunt
```

### 2.2 The relation table

| Relation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Father's/Mother's father** | Grandfather |
| **Father's/Mother's mother** | Grandmother |
| **Father's/Mother's brother** | Uncle |
| **Father's/Mother's sister** | Aunt |
| **Father's/Mother's son** | Brother (or self) |
| **Father's/Mother's daughter** | Sister (or self) |
| **Son's/Daughter's son** | Grandson |
| **Son's/Daughter's daughter** | Granddaughter |
| **Brother's/Sister's son** | Nephew |
| **Brother's/Sister's daughter** | Niece |
| **Uncle's/Aunt's son or daughter** | Cousin |
| **Husband's/Wife's father** | Father-in-law |
| **Husband's/Wife's mother** | Mother-in-law |
| **Husband's/Wife's brother** | Brother-in-law |
| **Husband's/Wife's sister** | Sister-in-law |
| **Son's wife** | Daughter-in-law |
| **Daughter's husband** | Son-in-law |
| **Grandfather's son** | Father or uncle |
| **Grandfather's only son** | Father |

> **The "only" qualifier is doing real work.** "My grandfather's **son**" could be your father or an uncle. "My grandfather's **only son**" must be your father. Examiners insert or omit "only" deliberately — read for it.

### 2.3 ⭐ The self-reference technique

The hardest questions are phrased as quotations: *"Pointing to a photograph, a man said…"*.

**The method: work from the INSIDE OUT, one clause at a time.**

> *"Pointing to a man, a woman said: 'His mother is the only daughter of my mother.'"*

```
Step 1:  Innermost phrase — "my mother"                → the WOMAN's mother
Step 2:  "the only daughter of my mother"              → the WOMAN herself
             (she is her mother's only daughter)
Step 3:  "His mother is [the woman]"                   → the woman IS the man's mother

  ⇒  The woman is the man's MOTHER
```

**Always resolve the innermost possessive first**, then work outwards. Reading left to right leads to confusion almost immediately.

> **The self-recognition move.** Phrases like "the only daughter of my mother" or "the only son of my father" almost always resolve to **the speaker**. Spotting this collapses the puzzle instantly.

### 2.4 Watch the speaker's gender

The speaker's gender is often the deciding fact.

> *"The only son of my father"* said by a **man** ⇒ himself
> *"The only son of my father"* said by a **woman** ⇒ her brother

Underline whether the speaker is "a man" or "a woman" before you begin.

### 2.5 Coded blood relations

Some questions define operators:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A **+** B | A is the father of B |
| A **−** B | A is the wife of B |
| A **×** B | A is the brother of B |
| A **÷** B | A is the sister of B |

**Method: translate left to right, one operator at a time, drawing as you go.**

> **P − Q + R** — how is R related to P?
```
P − Q  :  P is the wife of Q          →  P ═══ Q
Q + R  :  Q is the father of R        →  Q is R's parent

Since P is Q's wife, P is also R's parent
  ⇒  R is P's CHILD (son or daughter)
```

### 2.6 Family puzzles with extra attributes

When professions, ages or genders are attached, **build a table alongside the tree**.

```
Person │ Gender │ Generation │ Profession
───────┼────────┼────────────┼───────────
  F    │   M    │     1      │ Lawyer
  E    │   F    │     1      │ Housewife
  B    │   M    │     2      │ Doctor
  D    │   ?    │     3      │ Student
```

Fill what is directly stated first; deduce the rest.

## 3. Core Concepts — PART B: DIRECTION SENSE

### 3.1 The compass

```
                    NORTH (N)
                        │
          NW            │            NE
             ╲          │          ╱
               ╲        │        ╱
                 ╲      │      ╱
   WEST (W) ──────────  ●  ────────── EAST (E)
                 ╱      │      ╲
               ╱        │        ╲
             ╱          │          ╲
          SW            │            SE
                        │
                    SOUTH (S)
```

**Bearings (clockwise from north):**

| Direction | Bearing |
|---|---|
| North | 0° (or 360°) |
| North-east | 45° |
| East | 90° |
| South-east | 135° |
| South | 180° |
| South-west | 225° |
| West | 270° |
| North-west | 315° |

### 3.2 The turning rules

**This table is the whole topic. Memorise it.**

| Currently facing | Turn LEFT | Turn RIGHT |
|---|---|---|
| **North** | West | East |
| **East** | North | South |
| **South** | East | West |
| **West** | South | North |

**The mental model:** left is anticlockwise, right is clockwise.

```
        N
        ↑
   W ←──┼──→ E          Clockwise (RIGHT):  N → E → S → W → N
        ↓                Anticlockwise (LEFT): N → W → S → E → N
        S
```

> **The universal error:** applying "left = west" regardless of which way you are facing. Left is west **only when facing north**. Facing south, left is **east**.

### 3.3 Angular turns

| Turn | Effect |
|---|---|
| 45° | Half a compass point (N → NE) |
| 90° | One quarter turn (N → E clockwise) |
| 135° | N → SE clockwise |
| 180° | Complete reversal (N → S) |
| 270° | Three quarter turns (N → W clockwise) |
| 360° | Back to the start |

**Bearing arithmetic:**
$$\text{New bearing} = (\text{Old bearing} \pm \text{Turn}) \bmod 360°$$
*(+ for clockwise/right, − for anticlockwise/left.)*

> Facing **east** (90°), turn 135° clockwise:
> $$90 + 135 = 225° = \textbf{south-west}$$
> Then turn 180° anticlockwise:
> $$225 - 180 = 45° = \textbf{north-east}$$

### 3.4 ⭐ The coordinate method (use this for every multi-leg journey)

Place the start at the origin and treat each leg as a displacement.

$$\text{North} = +y \qquad \text{South} = -y \qquad \text{East} = +x \qquad \text{West} = -x$$

```
Journey:  20 m north, 30 m east, 35 m south, 15 m east, 15 m north

    Leg 1:  north 20   →  y = +20
    Leg 2:  east  30   →  x = +30
    Leg 3:  south 35   →  y = 20 − 35 = −15
    Leg 4:  east  15   →  x = 30 + 15 = +45
    Leg 5:  north 15   →  y = −15 + 15 = 0

    Final position: (45, 0)  ⇒  45 m due EAST of the start
```

**Track two running totals — one for north–south, one for east–west.** This eliminates the entire class of errors that comes from trying to visualise a five-turn path.

### 3.5 Shortest distance (displacement)

$$\text{Distance} = \sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2}$$

**Look for Pythagorean triples** — placement questions almost always use them:

| Δx, Δy | Distance |
|---|---|
| 3, 4 | **5** |
| 6, 8 | **10** |
| 5, 12 | **13** |
| 9, 12 | **15** |
| 8, 15 | **17** |
| 7, 7 | 7√2 ≈ 9.9 |
| 2, 2 | 2√2 ≈ 2.83 |

**Direction of the final position:**

| Δx | Δy | Direction from start |
|---|---|---|
| + | + | North-east |
| + | − | South-east |
| − | + | North-west |
| − | − | South-west |
| + | 0 | Due east |
| 0 | + | Due north |

### 3.6 Shadow problems

The sun rises in the **east** and sets in the **west**.

```
SUNRISE (morning):   sun in the EAST   →  shadows fall to the WEST
SUNSET  (evening):   sun in the WEST   →  shadows fall to the EAST
NOON:                sun overhead      →  no meaningful shadow direction
```

> *"At sunrise, a man's shadow falls to his right. Which way is he facing?"*
> At sunrise the shadow points **west**. If west is on his **right**, then he faces **north**.
>
> *(Stand facing north: your right hand points east, your left points west. Wait — check again. Facing north, your right is east and your left is west. So for west to be on his right, he must be facing **south**.)*

**The reliable procedure:**
```
1.  Determine the shadow's direction (west at sunrise, east at sunset)
2.  Place that direction where the question says it falls (right/left/front/behind)
3.  Read off the facing direction
```

Facing **south**: right = **west** ✓ left = east
Facing **north**: right = east, left = west

So at sunrise with the shadow on the right, the person faces **south**.

## 4. Shortcuts & Tricks

### 4.1 Blood relations — the four-step drill

```
1.  Note the SPEAKER'S GENDER (underline "a man" / "a woman")
2.  Resolve the INNERMOST possessive phrase first
3.  Draw the tree as you go — one line per clause
4.  Read the answer off the diagram, in the direction the question asks
```

### 4.2 Self-recognition phrases

| Phrase | Said by a man | Said by a woman |
|---|---|---|
| "The only son of my father" | Himself | Her brother |
| "The only daughter of my mother" | His sister | Herself |
| "My father's only child" | Himself | Herself |
| "My grandfather's only son" | His father | Her father |
| "The son of my mother" | Himself or his brother | Her brother |

### 4.3 Read the direction of the question

> *"How is **A** related to **B**?"* — you need A's role from B's perspective.

If A is B's mother's brother, then **A is B's maternal uncle** — not "B is A's nephew". The two are different answers and only one matches the question.

**Underline both names and note which comes first.**

### 4.4 Direction sense — the coordinate table

Never trace the path mentally. Tabulate.

```
Leg   Direction   Distance   x-total   y-total
───────────────────────────────────────────────
 1     North         20         0        +20
 2     East          30       +30        +20
 3     South         35       +30        −15
 4     East          15       +45        −15
 5     North         15       +45          0
                              ──────────────────
                    FINAL:    45 east,  0 north
```

### 4.5 The turning table — commit it to memory

```
Facing:   N     E     S     W
LEFT  →   W     N     E     S
RIGHT →   E     S     W     N
```

**Two-turn shortcuts:**
```
Right, right  →  180° reversal
Left, left    →  180° reversal
Left, right   →  back to the original direction
Right, right, right  →  same as one LEFT turn
```

### 4.6 Recognise the triples

Most direction questions are engineered so the final displacement forms a Pythagorean triple. If your Δx and Δy are 3 and 4, or 6 and 8, the answer is 5 or 10 — no square root needed.

If they are equal (say 7 and 7), the answer is **7√2 ≈ 9.9** and the direction is a diagonal (NE, NW, SE or SW).

### 4.7 Sanity checks

```
BLOOD RELATIONS
✓ Generations must be consistent — a grandchild cannot be older than a parent
✓ "Only son/daughter" eliminates siblings of that gender
✓ Check the DIRECTION of the question (A→B or B→A)
✓ Married couples connect two family branches — mark them clearly

DIRECTION SENSE
✓ Left/right depends on the CURRENT facing, not on north
✓ Opposite legs cancel: 20 north then 20 south = zero net movement
✓ Distance from the start uses Pythagoras, not the sum of the legs
✓ "How far" ≠ "how far did he walk" — the first is displacement, the second is path length
```

## 5. Solved Examples

### Example 1 — Simple family tree

**Q.** A is the father of B. B is the mother of C. C is the sister of D. How is A related to D?

**Step 1 — Draw the tree clause by clause.**

```
    A            A is B's father
    │
    B            B is C's mother
    │
  ┌─┴─┐
  C   D          C and D are siblings
```

**Step 2 — Trace the path from A to D.**
A → B (child) → C and D (grandchildren)

**Step 3 — Read off.**
D is two generations below A ⇒ **A is D's grandfather**.

**Answer: Grandfather**

> **Note the gender fact.** A is stated to be a *father*, so A is male ⇒ grandfather (not grandmother). D's gender is unknown and does not matter here.

---

### Example 2 — Self-reference (inside-out method)

**Q.** Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son." Whose photograph is it?

**Step 1 — Note the speaker's gender.** The speaker is a **man**.

**Step 2 — Resolve the innermost phrase.**
$$\text{"my father's son"}$$
The speaker has **no brothers**, so his father's only son is the speaker himself.
$$\implies \text{"my father's son"} = \textbf{the speaker}$$

**Step 3 — Substitute back.**
$$\text{"that man's father is [the speaker]"}$$

So the speaker is the father of the man in the photograph.

**Step 4 — Answer the question as asked.**
The photograph shows the speaker's **son**.

**Answer: His son**

```
        SPEAKER (man)
              │
        MAN IN PHOTO
```

> **The "no brother or sister" clause is not decoration.** It is what forces "my father's son" to be the speaker rather than an unnamed brother.

---

### Example 3 — Coded blood relations

**Q.** In a certain code:
- **A + B** means A is the father of B
- **A − B** means A is the wife of B
- **A × B** means A is the brother of B
- **A ÷ B** means A is the sister of B

If **P × Q + R ÷ S**, how is P related to S?

**Step 1 — Translate one operator at a time.**

```
P × Q  :  P is the BROTHER of Q
Q + R  :  Q is the FATHER of R
R ÷ S  :  R is the SISTER of S
```

**Step 2 — Build the tree.**

```
    P ─── Q                P and Q are brothers (P is male)
          │
       ┌──┴──┐
       R     S             R and S are Q's children
                           (R is female — she is S's sister)
```

**Step 3 — Locate P relative to S.**
Q is S's father. P is Q's brother. Therefore P is S's **father's brother**.

**Answer: P is S's uncle (paternal)**

> **Read the operators in sequence, not in isolation.** The chain P × Q + R ÷ S is read as three separate statements, each linking adjacent letters.

---

### Example 4 — Basic direction with a right-angled path

**Q.** A man walks 12 km towards the north, then turns right and walks 5 km. How far is he from his starting point, and in which direction?

**Step 1 — Set up coordinates.** Start at (0, 0).

**Step 2 — Track each leg.**

| Leg | Direction | Distance | x | y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | North | 12 | 0 | +12 |
| 2 | Right from north = **East** | 5 | +5 | +12 |

**Step 3 — Final position.**
$$(5, 12)$$

**Step 4 — Compute the distance (recognise the triple).**
$$\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13 \text{ km}$$

**Step 5 — Determine the direction.**
Both x and y are positive ⇒ **north-east** of the start.

**Answer: 13 km, in the north-east direction**

> **5-12-13 is the giveaway.** Placement questions overwhelmingly use 3-4-5, 6-8-10 and 5-12-13. Recognising the triple removes the square root entirely.

---

### Example 5 — Multi-leg journey with cancellation

**Q.** A person walks 10 m south, turns left and walks 15 m, turns left and walks 10 m, turns right and walks 5 m. How far and in which direction is he from the starting point?

**Step 1 — Work out each turn using the turning table.**

| Leg | Facing before | Turn | New facing | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | — | **South** | 10 m |
| 2 | South | Left | **East** | 15 m |
| 3 | East | Left | **North** | 10 m |
| 4 | North | Right | **East** | 5 m |

**Step 2 — Accumulate the coordinates.**

| Leg | Direction | x | y |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | South 10 | 0 | −10 |
| 2 | East 15 | +15 | −10 |
| 3 | North 10 | +15 | **0** |
| 4 | East 5 | **+20** | 0 |

**Step 3 — Read the result.**
Final position: **(20, 0)** ⇒ 20 m due **east** of the start.

**Answer: 20 m to the east**

> **The 10 m south and 10 m north cancel exactly**, leaving a purely east–west displacement. Watch for this pattern — it is deliberately built into many questions to reward the coordinate method and punish mental tracing.

---

### Example 6 — Angular turns

**Q.** A man is facing west. He turns 45° clockwise, then another 180° clockwise, and finally 270° anticlockwise. Which direction is he facing now?

**Step 1 — Convert the starting direction to a bearing.**
$$\text{West} = 270°$$

**Step 2 — Apply each turn.**

| Turn | Calculation | Bearing | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | — | 270° | West |
| +45° clockwise | 270 + 45 | 315° | North-west |
| +180° clockwise | 315 + 180 = 495 → 495 − 360 | 135° | South-east |
| −270° anticlockwise | 135 − 270 = −135 → −135 + 360 | **225°** | **South-west** |

**Answer: South-west**

> **Keep bearings in the range 0–360.** Add 360 when the result goes negative; subtract 360 when it exceeds 360. Doing this at every step prevents compounding errors.

---

### Example 7 — Complex self-reference

**Q.** Pointing to a lady in a photograph, Meena said, "Her father's only son's wife is my mother-in-law." How is Meena's husband related to the lady in the photograph?

**Step 1 — Resolve the innermost phrase.**
$$\text{"Her father's only son"} = \text{the lady's brother}$$

**Step 2 — Work outwards.**
$$\text{"Her father's only son's wife"} = \text{the lady's brother's wife} = \text{the lady's sister-in-law}$$

**Step 3 — Apply the given equality.**
That sister-in-law **is Meena's mother-in-law**, i.e. the mother of Meena's husband.

**Step 4 — Follow the chain to Meena's husband.**

```
     LADY  ───  LADY'S BROTHER ═══ HIS WIFE (= Meena's mother-in-law)
                       │                    │
                       └────────┬───────────┘
                                │
                        MEENA'S HUSBAND
```

Meena's husband is the son of the lady's brother.

**Step 5 — Name the relation.**
The lady is the sister of Meena's husband's father ⇒ she is his **aunt** (paternal), and he is her **nephew**.

**Answer: Meena's husband is the lady's nephew**

> **Check the direction of the question.** It asks how Meena's *husband* relates to the *lady* — so the answer is "nephew", not "aunt".

---

### Example 8 — Two people, one starting point

**Q.** Two friends start walking from the same point. Amit walks 6 km north and then 8 km east. Bina walks 8 km south and then 6 km west. How far apart are they?

**Step 1 — Compute Amit's final position.**

| Leg | Direction | x | y |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | North 6 | 0 | +6 |
| 2 | East 8 | **+8** | **+6** |

Amit is at **(8, 6)**.

**Step 2 — Compute Bina's final position.**

| Leg | Direction | x | y |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | South 8 | 0 | −8 |
| 2 | West 6 | **−6** | **−8** |

Bina is at **(−6, −8)**.

**Step 3 — Find the separation.**
$$\Delta x = 8 - (-6) = 14 \qquad \Delta y = 6 - (-8) = 14$$

**Step 4 — Apply Pythagoras.**
$$d = \sqrt{14^2 + 14^2} = \sqrt{392} = 14\sqrt{2} \approx 19.8 \text{ km}$$

**Answer: 14√2 ≈ 19.8 km apart**

> **Equal Δx and Δy always give a √2 answer** and a diagonal direction. Bina is exactly south-west of Amit here.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | — | **North** | 20 m |
| 2 | North | Right | **East** | 30 m |
| 3 | East | Right | **South** | 35 m |
| 4 | South | Left | **East** | 15 m |
| 5 | East | Left | **North** | 15 m |

**Step 2 — Accumulate the coordinates.**

| Leg | Direction | x | y |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | North 20 | 0 | +20 |
| 2 | East 30 | +30 | +20 |
| 3 | South 35 | +30 | −15 |
| 4 | East 15 | +45 | −15 |
| 5 | North 15 | **+45** | **0** |

**Step 3 — Read the result.**
Final position **(45, 0)** ⇒ **45 m due east** of the start.

**Answer: (b) 45 m east**

> **Both left turns matter.** After walking south, a left turn gives east; after walking east, a left turn gives north. Deriving each facing from the previous one — rather than assuming — is what makes this question tractable.

---

**Q43. "His wife is the only daughter of my father." → (c) Husband**

**Step 1 — Note the speaker's gender.** The speaker is a **woman**.

**Step 2 — Resolve the innermost phrase.**
$$\text{"the only daughter of my father"} = \textbf{the woman herself}$$
*(She is her father's only daughter.)*

**Step 3 — Substitute.**
$$\text{"His wife is [the woman]"}$$

$$\implies \text{The man is the woman's } \textbf{husband}$$

**Answer: (c) Husband**

---

**Q44. P is Q's brother; R is Q's daughter; S is P's sister. R to S? → (c) Niece**

**Step 1 — Build the tree.**
```
   P ─── Q ─── S            P, Q and S are all siblings
         │
         R                  R is Q's daughter
```

P and S are both siblings of Q (S is female — stated as P's sister, and she is also Q's sibling since P and Q are siblings).

**Step 2 — Locate R relative to S.**
R is the daughter of S's sibling ⇒ **niece**.

**Answer: (c) Niece**

---

**Q45. 5 km S, left 3 km, left 5 km, right 4 km. → (a) 7 km east**

**Step 1 — Determine the facing for each leg.**

| Leg | Facing before | Turn | New facing | Distance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | — | **South** | 5 km |
| 2 | South | Left | **East** | 3 km |
| 3 | East | Left | **North** | 5 km |
| 4 | North | Right | **East** | 4 km |

**Step 2 — Accumulate the coordinates.**

| Leg | Direction | x | y |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | South 5 | 0 | −5 |
| 2 | East 3 | +3 | −5 |
| 3 | North 5 | +3 | **0** |
| 4 | East 4 | **+7** | 0 |

**Step 3 — Read the result.**
Final position **(7, 0)** ⇒ **7 km due east**.

**Answer: (a) 7 km east**

> **Facing south, a LEFT turn gives EAST.** This is the single most-missed turn in the whole topic — the instinct to say "left = west" is strong and wrong.

---

**Q46. "She is the mother of my son's wife's daughter." (Ravi speaking) → (c) Daughter-in-law**

**Step 1 — Resolve from the inside out.**
$$\text{"my son's wife"} = \text{Ravi's DAUGHTER-IN-LAW}$$
$$\text{"my son's wife's daughter"} = \text{Ravi's GRANDDAUGHTER}$$

**Step 2 — Work outwards.**
$$\text{"the mother of [Ravi's granddaughter]"}$$

The granddaughter's mother is Ravi's son's wife — that is, Ravi's **daughter-in-law**.

```
    RAVI
      │
   RAVI'S SON ═══ HIS WIFE  ← the woman in the photograph
              │      (Ravi's daughter-in-law)
        GRANDDAUGHTER
```

**Answer: (c) Daughter-in-law**

> **The phrase loops back on itself.** Following the chain outward from the granddaughter returns you to the daughter-in-law you passed through on the way in — a deliberate feature of this question type.

---

**Q47. A is 2 km east of B; C is 3 km north of A; D is 5 km west of C. D from B? → (a) 3√2 km north-west**

**Step 1 — Place B at the origin and plot each point.**

| Point | Reasoning | Coordinates |
|---|---|---|
| B | Origin | (0, 0) |
| A | 2 km east of B | (2, 0) |
| C | 3 km north of A | (2, 3) |
| D | 5 km west of C | (2 − 5, 3) = **(−3, 3)** |

**Step 2 — Compute the distance from B.**
$$d = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2} \approx 4.24 \text{ km}$$

**Step 3 — Determine the direction.**
$$\Delta x = -3 \; (\text{west}), \qquad \Delta y = +3 \; (\text{north})$$

Negative x with positive y ⇒ **north-west**.

**Sketch:**
```
      D(−3,3) ●───────────────● C(2,3)
              ╲                │
               ╲               │ 3
          3√2   ╲              │
                 ╲             │
        B(0,0) ●───────────────● A(2,0)
                       2
```

**Answer: (a) 3√2 km north-west**

---

**Q48. B is a doctor and D's father; F is C's grandfather and a lawyer; E is D's grandmother and a housewife. D to F? → (c) Grandchild**

**Step 1 — Extract the stated facts.**

| Person | Fact | Gender |
|---|---|---|
| B | Doctor; father of D | Male |
| F | Grandfather of C; lawyer | Male |
| E | Grandmother of D; housewife | Female |
| D | Child of B | Unknown |

**Step 2 — Identify the married couple in generation 1.**
E is a grandmother and F is a grandfather. In a six-person family with two married couples, **E and F form the generation-1 couple**.

**Step 3 — Follow the chain to D.**
E is D's grandmother. Since F is E's husband, **F is D's grandfather**.

```
    F ═══ E            generation 1  (lawyer, housewife)
       │
       B ═══ ?         generation 2  (doctor)
          │
       ┌──┴──┐
       D     C         generation 3
```

**Step 4 — Answer the question as asked.**
$$D \text{ is F's } \textbf{grandchild}$$

*(D's gender is not stated, so "grandson" cannot be asserted — "grandchild" is the precise answer.)*

**Answer: (c) Grandchild**

---

**Q49. A: 3 km N then 4 km E. B: 4 km S then 3 km W. Distance apart? → (b) 7√2 km**

**Step 1 — Compute A's final position.**

| Leg | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| North 3 | 0 | +3 |
| East 4 | **+4** | **+3** |

A is at **(4, 3)**.

**Step 2 — Compute B's final position.**

| Leg | x | y |
|---|---|---|
| South 4 | 0 | −4 |
| West 3 | **−3** | **−4** |

B is at **(−3, −4)**.

**Step 3 — Compute the separation.**
$$\Delta x = 4 - (-3) = 7 \qquad \Delta y = 3 - (-4) = 7$$

**Step 4 — Apply Pythagoras.**
$$d = \sqrt{7^2 + 7^2} = \sqrt{98} = 7\sqrt{2} \approx 9.9 \text{ km}$$

**Answer: (b) 7√2 km**

> **Equal Δx and Δy always give a √2 answer.** When you see the two differences match, write the answer as k√2 immediately rather than computing √98.

---

**Q50. "His mother's only daughter is my mother." → (c) Niece**

**Step 1 — Resolve the innermost phrase.**
$$\text{"his mother's only daughter"} = \text{the MAN's sister}$$
*(The man's mother has exactly one daughter — the man's sister. The man himself is her son, not her daughter.)*

**Step 2 — Apply the given equality.**
$$\text{The man's sister} = \text{the WOMAN's mother}$$

**Step 3 — Build the tree.**
```
        MAN'S MOTHER
              │
        ┌─────┴─────┐
       MAN       HIS SISTER
                      │
                    WOMAN
```

**Step 4 — Name the relation.**
The woman's mother is the man's sister ⇒ the man is the woman's **maternal uncle**, and the woman is the man's **niece**.

**Step 5 — Check the direction of the question.**
The question asks how the **woman** is related to the **man** ⇒ **niece**.

**Answer: (c) Niece**

> **Direction discipline.** "Maternal uncle" is the correct answer to the *reverse* question ("how is the man related to the woman?"). Both relations are true; only one answers what was asked.

## 8. Quick Revision Sheet

> **One page. Read 48 hours after study, and again the night before the test.**

### BLOOD RELATIONS

```
NOTATION
    ○ female    □ male    △ unknown
    A ═══ B   married
    A                   parent above,
    │                   child below
    B
    A ─── B   siblings

METHOD
    1. Note the SPEAKER'S GENDER
    2. Resolve the INNERMOST possessive first
    3. Draw the tree, one clause at a time
    4. Read the answer in the DIRECTION the question asks
```

**Self-recognition phrases**

```
"The only son of my father"       man → himself     woman → her brother
"The only daughter of my mother"  man → his sister  woman → herself
"My father's only child"          → the speaker
"My grandfather's only son"       → the speaker's father
```

**Relation table**

```
Father's/Mother's father    → Grandfather
Father's/Mother's brother   → Uncle
Father's/Mother's sister    → Aunt
Brother's/Sister's son      → Nephew
Brother's/Sister's daughter → Niece
Son's wife                  → Daughter-in-law
Daughter's husband          → Son-in-law
Husband's/Wife's mother     → Mother-in-law
Uncle's/Aunt's child        → Cousin
```

**Coded relations**

```
A + B  →  A is the father of B
A − B  →  A is the wife of B
A × B  →  A is the brother of B
A ÷ B  →  A is the sister of B

Translate LEFT TO RIGHT, one operator at a time, drawing as you go.
```

### DIRECTION SENSE

```
⭐ THE TURNING TABLE (memorise)

    Facing:   N     E     S     W
    LEFT  →   W     N     E     S
    RIGHT →   E     S     W     N

BEARINGS (clockwise from north)
    N 0°   NE 45°   E 90°   SE 135°
    S 180° SW 225°  W 270°  NW 315°

    New bearing = (old ± turn) mod 360      + clockwise, − anticlockwise
```

**The coordinate method**

```
North = +y    South = −y    East = +x    West = −x

Tabulate every leg; keep TWO running totals.

Distance from start = √(Δx² + Δy²)

TRIPLES:  3,4→5   6,8→10   5,12→13   9,12→15   8,15→17
EQUAL:    7,7 → 7√2 ≈ 9.9      2,2 → 2√2 ≈ 2.83
```

**Final direction**

```
Δx    Δy    Direction
 +     +    North-east
 +     −    South-east
 −     +    North-west
 −     −    South-west
 +     0    Due east
 0     +    Due north
```

**Shadows**

```
SUNRISE : sun EAST  → shadow falls WEST
SUNSET  : sun WEST  → shadow falls EAST

Facing NORTH: right = east,  left = west
Facing SOUTH: right = west,  left = east
Facing EAST : right = south, left = north
Facing WEST : right = north, left = south
```

**Two-turn shortcuts**

```
Right + Right  =  180° reversal
Left  + Left   =  180° reversal
Left  + Right  =  back to the original
Right ×3       =  same as one LEFT turn
```

## 9. Common Mistakes

| # | Mistake | Example | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not drawing the tree** | Holding three generations mentally | Draw it — rough paper is allowed |
| 2 | **Reading a quotation left to right** | Starting from "His mother…" | Resolve the **innermost** phrase first |
| 3 | **Speaker's gender ignored** | "My father's only son" read as a brother | If the speaker is male, it is **him** |
| 4 | **"Only" overlooked** | "Grandfather's son" read as "only son" | "Only" eliminates siblings — read for it |
| 5 | **Question direction reversed** | Answering "uncle" when "niece" was asked | Underline both names; note the order |
| 6 | **Left = west assumed** | Facing south, turning left ⇒ west | Facing south, left = **east** |
| 7 | **Path length given as distance** | 3+4+3+4 = 14 km | Displacement is √(8² + 6²) = 10 km |
| 8 | **Cancelling legs missed** | Tracking 20 N and 20 S separately | They cancel — the net y is zero |
| 9 | **Bearing out of range** | Leaving a bearing at 495° | Subtract 360 ⇒ 135° |
| 10 | **Shadow direction reversed** | Sunrise shadow placed east | The sun is east ⇒ shadows fall **west** |
| 11 | **Gender asserted without evidence** | "Grandson" when gender is unstated | Answer "grandchild" |
| 12 | **Coupled branches not joined** | Treating a spouse as unrelated | A married couple shares all children |
