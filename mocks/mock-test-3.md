> **What makes Pattern C different.** The numerical section contains **no standalone arithmetic** — every question hangs off an exhibit. The verbal section abandons synonyms and grammar entirely in favour of strict evidence discipline. This is the format most candidates find unfamiliar, and it is worth practising precisely for that reason.

# SECTION 4 — SITUATIONAL JUDGEMENT
### 10 questions · 20 minutes

*Choose the **most effective** response.*

**Q51.** You are shadowing a senior on a client call and notice they have quoted a figure that you know from the file is out of date.
(a) Correct them on the call.
(b) Note it, and tell them immediately after the call so a correction can be issued promptly.
(c) Say nothing — it is their call.
(d) Email the client with the correct figure afterwards.

**Q52.** You are asked to complete a task by Friday but the person who must supply the input data is on leave until Thursday.
(a) Wait until Thursday and hope it can be done.
(b) Flag the dependency to your manager now, and ask whether the data can be obtained another way.
(c) Estimate the data yourself.
(d) Tell your manager on Thursday if it becomes a problem.

**Q53.** A client's junior staff member asks you to explain part of your methodology in detail.
(a) Refuse — methodology is confidential.
(b) Give a clear explanation at an appropriate level, and check with your manager if the request goes beyond what is normally shared.
(c) Explain everything in full detail.
(d) Redirect them to your manager without answering anything.

**Q54.** You are working on two engagements and both managers believe they have your full attention.
(a) Continue and hope neither notices.
(b) Tell both managers about the other commitment and ask them to agree your allocation.
(c) Prioritise the manager you report to formally.
(d) Prioritise whichever work is more interesting.

**Q55.** You are asked to review a colleague's work and find it substantially below standard.
(a) Approve it to avoid conflict.
(b) Provide specific, constructive feedback directly to the colleague, and escalate only if it is not addressed.
(c) Send it back with a note saying it is unacceptable.

(a) Raise it with your manager immediately.
(b) Mention it to the client contact first.

**Q57.** A recruitment event requires volunteers and you have capacity, but it is not part of your engagement work.
(a) Decline — it is not your job.

(a) Cancel your commitment without comment.

**Q59.** You realise you have been performing a routine check incorrectly for several weeks.
(a) Correct your method going forward and say nothing.

# DETAILED SOLUTIONS

## Section 1 — Numerical Reasoning

**Q1 → (c) ₹2,700 crore.** Regional totals: North 660, South 860, East 420, West 760.
$$660 + 860 + 420 + 760 = 2{,}700$$

**Q2 → (b) South.** South's annual total of **860** exceeds West (760), North (660) and East (420).

**Q3 → (c) 40%.** Quarterly totals: Q1 = 570, Q4 = 800.
$$\frac{800 - 570}{570} \times 100 = \frac{230}{570} \times 100 = 40.35\% \approx 40\%$$

**Q4 → (c) 32%.**
$$\frac{210}{660} \times 100 = 31.8\% \approx 32\%$$

**Q5 → (a) North.**

| Region | Q1 | Q4 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **North** | 120 | 210 | **75.0%** |
| South | 200 | 250 | 25.0% |
| East | 90 | 120 | 33.3% |
| West | 160 | 220 | 37.5% |

> **The elimination shortcut:** North has the *largest jump* (90) on the *smallest base* (120) — a strong candidate before any calculation. South has the same jump size as West but on a much larger base, so it can be discarded on sight.

**Q6 → (c) 1,480.**
$$520 + 300 + 480 + 180 = 1{,}480$$

**Q7 → (c) Advisory.**

| Function | 2022 | 2024 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audit | 400 | 520 | 30% |
| Tax | 250 | 300 | 20% |
| **Advisory** | 300 | 480 | **60%** |
| Support | 150 | 180 | 20% |

**Q8 → (c) 32%.**
$$\frac{480}{1{,}480} \times 100 = 32.4\% \approx 32\%$$

**Q9 → (c) 35%.** 2022 total = 1,100; 2024 total = 1,480.
$$\frac{380}{1{,}100} \times 100 = 34.5\% \approx 35\%$$

**Q10 → (b) 26 : 9.**
$$520 : 180 \implies \text{divide by 20} \implies 26 : 9$$

**Q11 → (c) ₹1,500 crore.**
$$0.30 \times 5{,}000 = 1{,}500$$

**Q12 → (b) 11.1%.** 2023 total = 1,250 + 1,150 + 900 + 700 + 500 = **4,500**.
$$\frac{5{,}000 - 4{,}500}{4{,}500} \times 100 = \frac{500}{4{,}500} \times 100 = 11.1\%$$

**Q13 → (a) P.**

| Company | 2023 | 2024 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** | 1,250 | 1,500 | **20.0%** |
| Q | 1,150 | 1,250 | 8.7% |
| R | 900 | 1,000 | 11.1% |
| S | 700 | 750 | 7.1% |

*(2024 revenues from the pie: P 30% = 1,500 · Q 25% = 1,250 · R 20% = 1,000 · S 15% = 750.)*

**Q14 → (c) 35%.**
$$20\% + 15\% = 35\%$$

**Q15 → (b) 2 : 1.**
$$1{,}500 : 750 = 2 : 1$$

*(Or directly from the shares: 30% : 15% = 2 : 1 — no conversion needed.)*

**Q16 → (c) ₹32 lakh.**
$$0.40 \times 80 = 32$$

**Q17 → (b) ₹12 lakh.**
$$\text{Contingency} = 100\% - (40 + 25 + 20)\% = 15\% \implies 0.15 \times 80 = 12$$

**Q18 → (c) ₹19.2 lakh.**
$$0.60 \times 32 = 19.2$$

> **Note the two-layer percentage.** The 60% applies to the *personnel* budget (₹32 lakh), not to the total. Applying it to ₹80 lakh would give ₹48 lakh — the intended trap.

**Q19 → (c) ₹4 lakh.**
$$(25\% - 20\%) \times 80 = 0.05 \times 80 = 4$$

*(Subtracting the percentages before multiplying halves the work.)*

**Q20 → (b) 5 : 3.**
$$25\% : 15\% = 5 : 3$$

## Section 2 — Verbal Reasoning

### Passage A

| Q | Statement | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **21** | 2,400 employees at end 2024 | **TRUE** | Stated verbatim |
| **22** | 2,200 employees at end 2023 | **TRUE** | 2,400 − 200 = 2,200; the arithmetic is required by "an increase of 200" |
| **23** | Nine locations | **TRUE** | *"now operates from nine locations"* |
| **24** | Three new cities | **FALSE** | The passage says **two** |
| **25** | Revenue per employee increased | **TRUE** | *"rose from ₹18 lakh to ₹19.5 lakh"* |
| **26** | Profitable in 2024 | **CANNOT SAY** | *"No information was published about profitability"* |
| **27** | Attrition was 12% | **TRUE** | Stated |
| **28** | 2023 attrition above 12% | **TRUE** | 12% is *"its lowest level in five years"*, so every prior year in that window — including 2023 — was higher |
| **29** | Largest firm in its market | **CANNOT SAY** | No comparative information |
| **30** | Total revenue increased | **TRUE** | Headcount rose **and** revenue per employee rose ⇒ their product necessarily rose |

> **Q28 and Q30 are the two that separate strong candidates.** Neither is stated directly, but both are **logically required** by what is stated. "Lowest in five years" carries information about the preceding four years. And a product of two increasing quantities must increase.
>
> **Q26 is the discipline test.** A firm reporting rising revenue per employee and falling attrition *sounds* successful — but profitability is explicitly absent from the passage. Cannot Say.

### Passage B

| Q | Statement | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **31** | 500 mid-sized manufacturers | **TRUE** | Stated |
| **32** | 62% adopted by 2024 | **TRUE** | Stated |
| **33** | Adoption rose 2021 → 2024 | **TRUE** | 41% → 62% |
| **34** | **All** firms >200 employees adopted | **FALSE** | The figure is **78%**, not 100% |
| **35** | 54% of **all** firms cited cost | **FALSE** | 54% of **non-adopters**, a much smaller group |
| **36** | The study found automation reduced employment | **FALSE** | *"The study did not examine the effect of automation on employment"* — it made no such finding |
| **37** | More than half had adopted | **TRUE** | 62% > 50% |
| **38** | Adoption lowest among the smallest firms | **CANNOT SAY** | The passage says adoption was *highest* among large firms; it does not rank the others |
| **39** | Fewer than half had adopted in 2021 | **TRUE** | 41% < 50% |
| **40** | The study was conducted in 2024 | **CANNOT SAY** | It *reports data up to* 2024; when it was conducted is not stated |

> **Q35 is the archetypal base error.** The 54% figure attaches to non-adopters (38% of the sample), so cost was cited by roughly 0.54 × 0.38 ≈ **21%** of all firms — not 54%. Always check which population a percentage is measured against.
>
> **Q36 vs Q38 shows the FALSE/CANNOT SAY boundary.** Q36 is FALSE because the passage *actively denies* that any such finding exists. Q38 is CANNOT SAY because the passage is simply *silent* on the ranking.

## Section 3 — Inductive Reasoning

**Q41 → (c) 112.** Each term doubles: 56 × 2 = **112**.

**Q42 → (c) The same as the start.**
$$3 \times 120° = 360° \equiv 0° \pmod{360°}$$

**Q43 → (c) 15.** Triangular numbers; the differences run 2, 3, 4, **5** ⇒ 10 + 5 = **15**.

**Q44 → (a) Shaded.** The cycle has period 3, with shading on positions 1, 4, 7, …
$$7 \bmod 3 = 1 \implies \textbf{shaded}$$

**Q45 → (c) 32.** Powers of 2: 16 × 2 = **32**.

**Q46 → (c) T.** Positions 2, 5, 9, 14 ⇒ differences 3, 4, 5 ⇒ next difference **6**:
$$14 + 6 = 20 = \textbf{T}$$

**Q47 → (c) q.** A vertical mirror flips left ↔ right: **p → q**.
*(The water image of p would be b.)*

**Q48 → (b) 1.** (n − 2)³ = 1³ = **1** — only the single central cube is hidden.

**Q49 → (b) 7.** Sides increase by one: 3, 4, 5, 6, **7** (a heptagon).

**Q50 → (c) 36.** Descending perfect squares: 10², 9², 8², 7², **6² = 36**.

## Section 4 — Situational Judgement

| Q | Ans | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **51** | b | Correct promptly **without** undermining a colleague in front of the client |
| **52** | b | Flag the dependency **early**, when it can still be worked around |
| **53** | b | Be helpful within normal bounds **and** check where the request exceeds them |
| **54** | b | Surface the conflict; let the managers allocate — it is their decision, not yours |
| **55** | b | Specific constructive feedback first; escalate only if unaddressed |
| **56** | **a** | **Regulatory concerns go to your manager immediately** — not to the client, not to peers, not into the file |
| **57** | b | Volunteer **and** confirm your commitments allow it |
| **58** | b | Respond promptly with a realistic time; neither martyrdom nor silence |
| **59** | b | Own the error, define the period affected, support the rework |
| **60** | b | Remove yourself and report; do not escalate to the client's HR yourself |

> **Q56 is the one that breaks the pattern.** In every other scenario the best answer includes a step of checking, consulting a peer, or gathering more information. A **potential regulatory breach** does not: it goes straight to your manager. Option (d) — discussing it with colleagues first — is well-intentioned but delays a matter that may have reporting obligations attached, and spreads sensitive information sideways rather than upward.
>
> **The general rule:** the escalation ladder has one exception. **Regulatory, ethical and independence concerns skip the peer-consultation rung.**

## Post-Test Review

```
□  Section 1: were errors from ARITHMETIC or from READING THE EXHIBIT?
       Reading errors  →  practise the 20-second exhibit scan
       Arithmetic      →  drill the fraction–percentage table

□  Section 2: how many did you mark CANNOT SAY?
       Fewer than 3 out of 20  →  you are importing outside knowledge
       More than 8             →  you are under-using logical necessity
                                  (Q22, Q28 and Q30 are all derivable)

□  Section 3: any errors were pure recall — revisit the pattern bank

□  Section 4: did you spot that Q56 breaks the pattern?
```

**The single most valuable takeaway from Pattern C practice:** in the verbal section, the answer depends *only* on the passage. Statements 26, 29, 38 and 40 are all things a reasonable person might believe — and all four are Cannot Say.
