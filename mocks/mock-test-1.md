# SECTION 1 — SITUATIONAL STRENGTH
### 20 questions · 15 minutes · ~45 seconds per question

*For each scenario, choose the response you consider **most effective**.*

---

**Q1.** You are told by a senior that a figure in a client schedule is "close enough" to proceed, but your recalculation shows a material difference.
(a) Proceed as instructed — the senior has more experience.
(b) Ask the senior to explain their assessment, showing your recalculation; escalate to the manager if the concern remains.
(c) Amend the schedule yourself before the review.
(d) Note your disagreement privately and proceed.

**Q2.** A colleague asks you to cover for them by saying they were in a meeting when they were actually late.
(a) Agree — it is a small matter.
(b) Decline politely and suggest they explain the lateness themselves.
(c) Agree, but tell them not to ask again.
(d) Report the request to your manager immediately.

**Q3.** You are two days into a five-day task and realise you will need seven days.
(a) Work longer hours to compress it into five.
(b) Inform your manager now with a revised estimate and options.
(c) Continue and inform them on day five.
(d) Reduce the depth of the work to fit five days.

**Q4.** A client emails you directly with a technical question that your manager normally handles.
(a) Answer it to be responsive.
(b) Acknowledge receipt, tell the client you will come back promptly, and forward it to your manager.
(c) Ignore it and wait for your manager to notice.
(d) Forward it to your manager without replying to the client.

**Q5.** During a team meeting you realise a decision is being made on the basis of data you know to be out of date.
(a) Say nothing — you are the most junior person present.
(b) Raise the point factually and briefly at the appropriate moment.
(c) Send an email after the meeting.
(d) Mention it to one colleague afterwards.

**Q6.** A colleague on a different engagement asks to see a client file you are working on, out of curiosity.
(a) Share it — you work at the same firm.
(b) Decline, explaining that access is restricted to the engagement team.
(c) Share a redacted version.
(d) Ask your manager whether it is acceptable, and follow their direction.

**Q7.** You have finished your work for the day and the team is visibly under pressure.
(a) Leave — your work is done.
(b) Tell the senior you have capacity and ask what would be most useful.
(c) Start helping with whatever looks most urgent.
(d) Offer to help tomorrow instead.

**Q8.** You are given feedback that your written work is too detailed and needs to be more concise.
(a) Explain that the detail is necessary for accuracy.
(b) Acknowledge the feedback, ask for an example of the right level, and apply it to your next piece.
(c) Reduce the detail sharply on everything going forward.
(d) Ask a different reviewer for a second opinion.

**Q9.** A junior colleague makes an error that you notice before the reviewer does.
(a) Correct it silently.
(b) Point it out to them directly, explain the issue, and let them correct it.
(c) Tell the reviewer.
(d) Leave it for the review process to catch.

**Q10.** You are asked to work on an engagement for a company whose shares you personally hold.
(a) Accept — your holding is small.
(b) Disclose the holding to your manager and the independence team before accepting.
(c) Sell the shares and accept.
(d) Decline without explanation.

**Q11.** Two tasks are due at the same time: an internal template update and a client deliverable.
(a) Complete the internal task first as it is quicker.
(b) Prioritise the client deliverable and inform your manager the internal task will be delayed.
(c) Attempt both at reduced quality.
(d) Ask a colleague to take the internal task without checking with your manager.

**Q12.** A client contact tells you something in confidence that is relevant to your engagement conclusions.
(a) Keep it confidential as requested.
(b) Explain that information relevant to the engagement must be shared with your team, and discuss how to handle it.
(c) Share it with your team without telling the client.
(d) Ignore the information.

**Q13.** You disagree with a process your team has followed for years, believing it inefficient.
(a) Continue following it without comment.
(b) Raise your observation with your manager, with a specific alternative and its benefits.
(c) Adopt your own approach for your portion of the work.
(d) Discuss it with peers but not with management.

**Q14.** Your manager gives you critical feedback in front of the team.
(a) Defend yourself immediately.
(b) Accept it professionally in the moment, and if you wish to discuss the manner, raise it privately later.
(c) Say nothing and disengage from the team.
(d) Complain about the manager to a colleague.

**Q15.** You notice a colleague appears to be struggling with workload and stress.
(a) Say nothing — it is not your business.
(b) Check in with them privately and, if appropriate, encourage them to speak to the manager.
(c) Tell the manager immediately.
(d) Offer to take over their work.

**Q16.** A supplier to your client offers you a discounted personal purchase because of your role.
(a) Accept — it is unrelated to your work.
(b) Decline and inform your manager.
(c) Accept but disclose it later.
(d) Ask a colleague whether it is acceptable.

**Q17.** You are unsure how to complete part of a task and your manager is unavailable.
(a) Guess and proceed.
(b) Ask an appropriate colleague, explain what you have tried, and continue with the parts you can do.
(c) Stop work until your manager returns.
(d) Complete only the parts you are sure about, without asking.

**Q18.** A deliverable is due tomorrow and you realise a key assumption was never confirmed with the client.
(a) Proceed with your assumption and note it in the file.
(b) Raise it with your manager now so the assumption can be confirmed before delivery.
(c) Contact the client directly to confirm.
(d) Change the assumption to a more conservative one.

**Q19.** During a client meeting, a colleague states something you believe is factually incorrect.
(a) Correct them in front of the client.
(b) Note it, and raise it with them immediately after the meeting so any correction can be made promptly.
(c) Say nothing at all.
(d) Email the client afterwards with the correct position.

**Q20.** You have been asked to do a repetitive task you find tedious and below your capability.
(a) Do it to a minimum standard.
(b) Complete it properly, and separately discuss with your manager the kind of work you would like to develop into.
(c) Ask to be reassigned.
(d) Delegate it to someone more junior.

# DETAILED SOLUTIONS

## Section 1 — Situational Strength

Every scenario in this section is resolved by the **four-step ladder** from [Topic 22](../topics/22-situational-judgement.md):

$$\text{Gather information} \to \text{Consult} \to \text{Act within authority} \to \text{Escalate transparently}$$

| Q | Answer | Competency | Why the answer is correct | Why the others fail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **b** | Integrity under hierarchy | Asks the senior to explain, showing evidence; escalates one rung if unresolved | (a) deference over judgement · (c) unilateral amendment · (d) documents without solving |
| **2** | **b** | Integrity | Declines an untruth, redirects responsibility to the colleague | (a)(c) participate in a falsehood · (d) disproportionate for a first request |
| **3** | **b** | Prioritisation + communication | Early warning with a revised estimate lets the manager replan | (a) heroic, hides the issue · (c) too late · (d) quality reduced silently |
| **4** | **b** | Client focus + authority | Responsive to the client **and** routes correctly | (a) exceeds authority · (c) unresponsive · (d) leaves the client waiting |
| **5** | **b** | Courage to lead | Seniority governs *how* you raise a point, not *whether* | (a) lets a wrong decision stand · (c)(d) too late |
| **6** | **b** | Confidentiality | Engagement-team access is a defined boundary | (a)(c) disclosure regardless of form · (d) asking is unnecessary here — the rule is clear |
| **7** | **b** | Teaming + initiative | Offers capacity where the team needs it | (a) withdraws · (c) may duplicate effort · (d) unhelpful timing |
| **8** | **b** | Accountability | Accepts feedback and seeks the calibration point | (a) defensive · (c) over-corrects · (d) shops for a better answer |
| **9** | **b** | Teaming | Lets the colleague learn and correct their own work | (a) they never learn · (c) disproportionate · (d) wastes reviewer time |
| **10** | **b** | Independence | Disclosure is mandatory; the firm decides | (a) appearance matters · (c) pre-empts the firm's judgement · (d) gives no information |
| **11** | **b** | Prioritisation | Client deadline first **and** communicate the slip | (a) wrong priority · (c) quality failure · (d) reallocates without authority |
| **12** | **b** | Client focus + integrity | Cannot accept engagement-relevant information off the record | (a) withholds from the team · (c) misleads the client · (d) discards relevant evidence |
| **13** | **b** | Initiative | Constructive observation with a specific alternative | (a) withholds value · (c) unilateral divergence · (d) generates dissatisfaction without change |
| **14** | **b** | Professionalism | Separates the substance (accept now) from the manner (raise later, privately) | (a) escalates publicly · (c) disengages · (d) undermines the manager |
| **15** | **b** | Teaming + respect | Private check-in first; escalation only if warranted | (a) indifferent · (c) escalates before speaking to them · (d) overreach |
| **16** | **b** | Integrity | A benefit arising from your role creates an independence concern | (a)(c) accept the benefit · (d) delays a clear decision |
| **17** | **b** | Communication | Asks the right person **and** keeps the work moving | (a) guessing · (c) stops entirely · (d) leaves the gap unaddressed |
| **18** | **b** | Client focus + authority | The assumption must be confirmed, through the right channel | (a) delivers on an unverified basis · (c) exceeds authority · (d) changes substance without agreement |

## Section 2 — Numerical Ability

---

**Q21. 45% of 720 → (c) 324**

**Mental method:**
```
10% of 720 = 72
40%        = 288
 5%        = 36
──────────────────
45%        = 324
```

**Answer: (c) 324**

---

**Q22. ₹1,200 marked, 25% discount → (c) ₹900**

25% off means you pay **75%**:
$$1200 \times 0.75 = 900$$

> Option (a) ₹850 and (b) ₹880 are near-misses; option (d) ₹960 is 20% off — a common misread of the discount rate.

**Answer: (c) ₹900**

---

**Q23. A in 15 days, B in 10 days. Together → (b) 6 days**

**LCM method:** Work = LCM(15, 10) = 30 units
$$A = 2 \text{ units/day}, \quad B = 3 \text{ units/day} \implies \text{combined} = 5$$
$$\frac{30}{5} = 6 \text{ days}$$

**Formula check:** (15 × 10)/25 = 6 ✓

**Answer: (b) 6 days**

---

**Q24. Average of 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 → (b) 24**

These form an arithmetic progression, so the average is the **middle term**: **24**.

**Check:** Sum = 120; 120/5 = 24 ✓

**Answer: (b) 24**

---

**Q25. SI on ₹8,000 at 6% for 2.5 years → (c) ₹1,200**

$$SI = \frac{8000 \times 6 \times 2.5}{100} = 80 \times 6 \times 2.5 = 1{,}200$$

**Answer: (c) ₹1,200**

---

**Q26. 180 m train at 72 km/h crossing a 220 m platform → (c) 20 s**

**Step 1 — Convert the speed.**
$$72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20 \text{ m/s}$$

**Step 2 — Total distance = train + platform.**
$$180 + 220 = 400 \text{ m}$$

**Step 3 — Time.**
$$\frac{400}{20} = 20 \text{ seconds}$$

**Answer: (c) 20 s**

---

**Q27. Ratio 5 : 8, difference 27. Larger number → (d) 72**

**Step 1 — Difference in parts.**
$$8 - 5 = 3 \text{ parts} = 27 \implies 1 \text{ part} = 9$$

**Step 2 — Larger number.**
$$8 \times 9 = 72$$

**Check:** 45 and 72; difference = 27 ✓

**Answer: (d) 72**

---

**Q28. Total revenue → (c) ₹1,200 crore**

$$240 + 360 + 180 + 420 = 1{,}200$$

**Answer: (c) ₹1,200 crore**

---

**Q29. Division B as a percentage of total → (c) 30%**

$$\frac{360}{1200} \times 100 = 30\%$$

**Answer: (c) 30%**

---

**Q30. Ratio of D to C → (c) 7 : 3**

$$420 : 180$$
Divide both by 60:
$$7 : 3$$

**Answer: (c) 7 : 3**

---

**Q31. Price +20%, consumption −10%. Change in expenditure → (a) 8% increase**

**Expenditure = Price × Consumption**, so multiply the factors:
$$1.20 \times 0.90 = 1.08 \implies \textbf{8\% increase}$$

**Verification:** 100 × 100 = 10,000 → 120 × 90 = 10,800 ⇒ +8% ✓

**Answer: (a) 8% increase**

---

**Q32. CI on ₹5,000 at 10% for 2 years → (b) ₹1,050**

$$A = 5000 \times (1.10)^2 = 5000 \times 1.21 = ₹6{,}050$$
$$CI = 6{,}050 - 5{,}000 = ₹1{,}050$$

> Option (a) ₹1,000 is the **simple** interest — the standard distractor.
> The ₹50 difference is exactly 10% of the first year's ₹500 interest.

**Answer: (b) ₹1,050**

## Section 3 — Verbal Ability

---

**Q33. CONCUR → (b) Agree**

**CONCUR** means to be of the same opinion; to agree.
*(Root: con- = together, currere = to run — "to run together".)*

**Answer: (b) Agree**

---

**Q34. Antonym of SCARCE → (b) Abundant**

SCARCE = in short supply. Options (a) Rare, (c) Limited and (d) Insufficient are all **synonyms**.

**Answer: (b) Abundant**

---

**Q35. "Each of the students have submitted…" → (b)**

**Rule:** *each* takes a **singular** verb.
$$\text{Each of the students } \textbf{has} \text{ submitted}$$

**Answer: (b)**

---

**Q36. "The report was ______ detailed that…" → (b) So**

**Rule:** the correlative pair is **so … that** (with an adjective) and **such … that** (with a noun).
$$\text{so detailed that} \; ✓ \qquad \text{such a detailed report that} \; ✓$$

**Answer: (b) So**

---

**Q37. Correct sentence → (a) He is taller than his brother.**

*Taller* is already comparative; adding "more" is a double comparative.

**Answer: (a)**

---

**Q38. MITIGATE → (b) Reduce**

MITIGATE means to make less severe. Option (a) Worsen is the antonym.

**Answer: (b) Reduce**

---

**Q39. A speech without preparation → (a) Impromptu**

**Answer: (a) Impromptu**

---

**Q40. "To turn a blind eye" → (a) To ignore something deliberately**

**Answer: (a)**

---

**Q41. "The company have announced…" → (b)**

**Rule:** collective nouns take a **singular** verb when acting as one unit — and the sentence itself confirms this with "**its** results".
$$\text{The company } \textbf{has} \text{ announced its results}$$

**Answer: (b)**

---

**Q42. "…working here ______ 2019." → (b) Since**

**Rule:** *since* takes a **point** in time; *for* takes a duration.

**Answer: (b) Since**

---

**Q43. Antonym of TRANSPARENT → (b) Opaque**

**Answer: (b) Opaque**

---

**Q44. Para jumble → (a) QSPR**

**Opener:** Q introduces the topic ("The department introduced a new digital workflow"). P and R both begin with back-references ("This", "as a result"); S describes a detail of the change.

**Chain:**
```
Q  →  the change was introduced
S  →  what the change involved (manual → automated)
P  →  the immediate effect ("This has reduced processing time")
R  →  the further consequence ("as a result")
```

**Answer: (a) QSPR**

## Section 4 — Logical Reasoning

---

**Q45. 5, 11, 23, 47, ? → (c) 95**

**Differences:** 6, 12, 24 — doubling ⇒ test **×2 + 1**:
$$5(2)+1 = 11 ✓ \quad 11(2)+1 = 23 ✓ \quad 23(2)+1 = 47 ✓$$
$$47 \times 2 + 1 = \mathbf{95}$$

**Answer: (c) 95**

---

**Q46. A, D, G, J, ? → (c) M**

**Positions:** 1, 4, 7, 10 ⇒ +3 each time
$$10 + 3 = 13 = \textbf{M}$$
