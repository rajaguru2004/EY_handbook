# SECTION 1 — ENGLISH / VERBAL ABILITY
### 12 questions · 18 minutes

**Directions for Q1–Q4:** Read the passage and answer the questions.

> Organisations increasingly measure what is easy to count rather than what matters. A support team's performance may be judged on tickets closed per hour, a metric that is precise, automatically captured and entirely silent on whether the customer's problem was actually resolved. The measure becomes the objective, and behaviour reshapes itself around it.
>
> The phenomenon is well documented. When a target becomes the definition of success, people optimise for the target. Tickets are closed prematurely and reopened later; the count rises while the underlying service deteriorates. The organisation's dashboard shows improvement precisely as its customers grow more frustrated.
>
> The remedy is not to abandon measurement but to pair each easily-counted metric with a harder one that captures the outcome. Tickets closed per hour, considered alongside reopening rate and customer-reported resolution, becomes informative rather than misleading. The difficulty is that the second measure is always more expensive to obtain, which is precisely why it is so often omitted.

# DETAILED SOLUTIONS

## Section 1 — English / Verbal

**Q1 → (b) What is easy to count.** Opening line: *"Organisations increasingly measure what is easy to count rather than what matters."*

**Q2 → (b) People optimise for the target.** *"When a target becomes the definition of success, people optimise for the target."*

**Q3 → (b) Pair each easy metric with a harder one.** *"The remedy is not to abandon measurement but to pair each easily-counted metric with a harder one that captures the outcome."* Option (a) is explicitly ruled out by "not to abandon measurement".

**Q4 → (b) More expensive to obtain.** *"…the second measure is always more expensive to obtain, which is precisely why it is so often omitted."*

**Q5 → (b) Practical.** PRAGMATIC = dealing with things sensibly and realistically. Option (a) Idealistic and (c) Theoretical are near-antonyms.

**Q6 → (b) Inconspicuous.** CONSPICUOUS = clearly visible, standing out. Options (a), (c) and (d) are all synonyms.

**Q7 → (c).** With **neither…nor**, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. The nearer subject is "team members" (plural), so the correct form is **were informed**, not "was informed".

**Q8 → (b) Submitted.** "Last Friday" fixes the tense as simple past. The collective noun "committee" takes a singular verb, and "submitted" is correct for both number and tense.

**Q9 → (c) Both are acceptable in modern usage.** *Data* is historically the plural of *datum* and takes "suggest"; in contemporary usage it is widely treated as a mass noun taking "suggests". Both forms appear in professional writing.

**Q10 → (b) Panacea.** A universal remedy. *(Antidote* counteracts a specific poison; *placebo* has no active effect; *vaccine* prevents a specific disease.)

**Q11 → (a) To change the rules or criteria unfairly during a process.**

**Q12 → (a) QSPR.** Q opens (introduces the framework and its date). S describes what it contained. P gives the subsequent spread ("since"). R adds the critical qualification ("however").

## Section 2 — Quantitative Aptitude

**Q13 → (b) 10%.**
$$\frac{7260}{6000} = 1.21 = (1.10)^2 \implies R = 10\%$$

**Q14 → (b) 34.** For consecutive terms, the average is the middle term ⇒ 30 is the third. The five numbers are 26, 28, **30**, 32, 34.

**Q15 → (a) 5% profit.**
$$1.40 \times 0.75 = 1.05 \implies 5\% \text{ profit}$$

**Q16 → (c) 9 min.** Capacity = LCM(12, 18, 36) = 36 units.
$$A = +3, \quad B = +2, \quad C = -1 \implies \text{net} = +4 \text{ units/min}$$
$$\frac{36}{4} = 9 \text{ minutes}$$

**Q17 → (c) 144.** PENCIL has 6 letters: vowels **E, I** (2) and consonants **P, N, C, L** (4).

The odd positions are **1, 3 and 5** — three slots, but only two vowels. So the vowels take two of the three odd slots, and the four consonants fill the remaining four positions.

$$\text{Place the 2 vowels in 3 odd slots} = \;^3P_2 = 6$$
$$\text{Arrange the 4 consonants in the remaining 4 slots} = 4! = 24$$
$$\text{Total} = 6 \times 24 = \mathbf{144}$$

> **The trap here** is assuming the number of vowels equals the number of odd positions. It does not — one odd position is taken by a consonant, which is why the vowel placement is a *permutation* ³P₂ rather than a simple 2!.

**Q18 → (c) 4.5 km/h.**
$$\text{Downstream} = \frac{30}{2} = 15 \text{ km/h}, \qquad \text{Upstream} = \frac{18}{3} = 6 \text{ km/h}$$
$$\text{Stream} = \frac{\text{Down} - \text{Up}}{2} = \frac{15 - 6}{2} = 4.5 \text{ km/h}$$

*(And the boat in still water = (15 + 6)/2 = 10.5 km/h.)*

**Q19 → (c) 77.** LCM(12, 18, 24) = 72 ⇒ N = 72 + 5 = **77**.

**Q20 → (a) ₹5,900 lakh.**

| Grade | Employees × Salary | Bill (₹ lakh) |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst | 200 × 8 | 1,600 |
| Senior | 120 × 15 | 1,800 |
| Manager | 60 × 25 | 1,500 |
| Director | 20 × 50 | 1,000 |
| **Total** | **400 employees** | **5,900** |

**Q21 → (b) ₹14.75 lakh.**
$$\frac{5{,}900}{400} = ₹14.75 \text{ lakh}$$

> **Note the weighting.** The simple mean of 8, 15, 25 and 50 is ₹24.5 lakh — offered as option (d). Analysts make up half the headcount, pulling the true average down to ₹14.75 lakh.

**Q22 → (c) 16.9%.**
$$\frac{1{,}000}{5{,}900} \times 100 = 16.95\% \approx 16.9\%$$

Directors are **5%** of the headcount but nearly **17%** of the payroll — the kind of disproportion cross-exhibit DI questions are designed to surface.

**Q23 → (b) 35 years.**
$$\frac{7x + 10}{2x + 10} = \frac{9}{4} \implies 28x + 40 = 18x + 90 \implies x = 5$$
$$\text{Father} = 7 \times 5 = 35 \text{ years}$$

**Q24 → (b) 19/66.**
$$\text{Total ways} = \;^{12}C_2 = 66$$
$$\text{Same colour} = \;^5C_2 + \;^4C_2 + \;^3C_2 = 10 + 6 + 3 = 19$$
$$P = \frac{19}{66}$$

## Section 3 — Reasoning Ability

**Q25 → (b) 42.** Differences 4, 6, 8, 10 ⇒ next is 12 ⇒ 30 + 12 = **42**. *(These are n(n+1): 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 42.)*

**Q26 → (a) OQVJGT.** Shift is **+2**: M→O, O→Q, T→V, H→J, E→G, R→T.

**Q27 → (b) Daughter.**

**Step 1 — Resolve the innermost phrase.** The speaker is a **man**, and "my father's **only** son" must therefore be **the speaker himself**.

**Step 2 — Substitute.** "She is the daughter of [the speaker]" ⇒ she is **his daughter**.

> **Option (a) Sister is the planted trap.** "The daughter of my father" would be his sister — but the phrase is "the daughter of my father's only son", one generation further down.
