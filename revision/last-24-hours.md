# The Last 24 Hours

### EY Placement Aptitude Handbook · Night-Before & Test-Day Checklist

> **The single most important instruction in this document: do not learn anything new today.**
>
> Nothing you begin studying in the final 24 hours will be reliably available under time pressure tomorrow. What *will* help is consolidating what you already know, resting properly, and arriving with a clear plan for how you will spend your 42 (or 82) minutes.

## 1. The Day Before — Hour by Hour

**A realistic plan for the final day. Total study time: about four hours.**

| Time | Activity | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning** | Read the [Master Formula Sheet](master-formula-sheet.md) end to end, without stopping to practise | 60 min |
| | Break — go outside | 20 min |
| | Read the [Shortcuts & Tables](shortcuts-and-tables.md), covering the right column and self-testing | 45 min |
| **Midday** | **Lunch and a genuine break.** No revision. | 90 min |
| **Afternoon** | Attempt **one** timed section only (numerical or reasoning) from a mock you have already done | 15 min |
| | Read [Topic 22 — Situational Judgement](../topics/22-situational-judgement.md) Sections 3 and 8 | 30 min |
| | Read the "Quick Revision Sheet" of your **three weakest** topics only | 45 min |
| **Evening** | Logistics check (Section 3 below) | 20 min |
| | **Stop.** Close the handbook. | — |
| **Night** | Normal evening. Sleep at your usual time. | — |

> **Why so little practice?** Practice at this stage cannot build new capability, but it *can* dent your confidence if a question goes badly. Reading consolidates; testing risks. On the final day, read.

## 3. Logistics Checklist

### For an online / remote test

```
□  Confirm the exact DATE and TIME (and time zone) of your slot
□  Confirm the platform and whether a system check is required
□  Run the system check TODAY, not tomorrow
□  Test your webcam and microphone if the test is proctored
□  Check your internet connection; arrange a backup (mobile hotspot)
□  Fully charge your laptop AND locate the charger
□  Clear your desk — most proctored tests require an empty workspace
□  Confirm what you are permitted: rough sheets? pen? calculator?
□  Have your ID document ready (usually a government photo ID)
□  Tell everyone in the house the time window and ask not to be disturbed
□  Close every other application and browser tab
□  Disable notifications on your laptop and phone
```

### For an in-person test

```
□  Confirm the venue address and plan the route
□  Plan to arrive 45 minutes early — traffic is not an excuse the system accepts
□  Print or download your admit card / call letter
□  Carry your ID document
□  Carry two working pens and a pencil
□  Carry a simple watch (many venues prohibit phones and smartwatches)
□  Check whether a calculator is permitted (usually not)
□  Note the reporting time versus the test start time — they differ
```

### Both formats

```
□  Read the instructions you were sent, in full, tonight
□  Note the SECTIONAL time limits for your pattern
□  Note whether back-navigation within a section is allowed
□  Confirm there is no negative marking (there normally is not)
□  Set two alarms
□  Lay out clothes, ID and materials tonight
```

## 4. The Morning Of

```
□  Wake at your normal time — not earlier
□  Eat a normal breakfast. Do not skip it.
□  Drink water. Dehydration measurably impairs arithmetic.
□  Do NOT attempt new practice questions
□  Read the ONE-PAGE CARD (Section 8) once — five minutes, no more
□  Arrive / log in early
□  Use the waiting time to breathe, not to revise
```

**If you feel anxious:** this is normal and does not predict performance. Slow your breathing deliberately — four counts in, six counts out, for two minutes. The physiological effect is real and takes effect within about ninety seconds.

## 5. In the Test — Section Playbook

### Before the first question

```
1.  Read the on-screen instructions in full — they override anything in
    this handbook. Patterns change.
2.  Confirm: how many sections? sectional timing? back-navigation allowed?
3.  Note the number of questions and the time for the section you are in.
4.  Compute your per-question budget:  time ÷ questions.
```

### Numerical Ability

```
□  Scan the OPTIONS before solving — widely spaced options mean approximate
□  For DI: spend 20 seconds reading UNITS and axis labels before any question
□  Within a DI set, do the DIRECT READ questions first, comparisons last
□  Watch the base in every percentage change — it is the OLD value
□  If a question needs a quadratic, check the options by substitution instead
□  75-second hard limit per question
```

### Verbal Ability

```
□  Read RC QUESTIONS first, then the passage
□  Never read a passage twice in full — return to specific lines
□  In True/False/Cannot Say: expect 3–4 "Cannot Say" out of 8
□  Eliminate options containing all · never · only · proves
□  Trust the fifteen grammar rules over what "sounds right"
```

### Logical Reasoning

```
□  DRAW. Blood relations, directions and seating are error-prone in your head
□  Circular puzzles: write "IN" or "OUT" in the middle before placing anyone
□  Facing the centre → clockwise is each person's LEFT
□  "k people between" means a gap of k + 1
□  Puzzle resisting after 2 minutes → flag it and move on
□  Series: verify your rule against EVERY given term before selecting
```

### Situational Judgement

```
□  Answer as a capable FIRST-YEAR ASSOCIATE
□  Eliminate: "without telling anyone" · "quietly" · "ignore" · "wait and see"
□  Eliminate: contacting the client directly · going over your manager first
□  The best answer usually does TWO things: acts AND communicates
□  Be CONSISTENT across scenarios — consistency is itself scored
□  Do not over-think. 45–60 seconds each, then move.
```

### The final 60 seconds of every section

```
1.  STOP solving.
2.  Scroll through and check that NO question is unanswered.
3.  Fill every blank with a guess. One letter is as good as any.
4.  Only then, if time remains, return to a flagged question.
```

## 7. If Things Go Wrong

**A section went badly.**
Sections are scored independently and you cannot change one that has closed. Dwelling on it costs you the next section too. Take one breath, note that it is finished, and start the new section as though it were the first.

**You ran out of time and left blanks.**
It happens. Note *why* — did you sink time into one question, or were you slow throughout? — and correct it in the next section. If back-navigation is disabled, you cannot fix it now.

**You froze on a question.**
Move on immediately. Do not attempt to recover by force. The next question is almost certainly easier, and answering it restores your rhythm faster than solving the hard one would.

**You realise you misread the instructions.**
If the section is still open and back-navigation is allowed, fix what you can in the time remaining. If not, apply the correct approach from here on and accept the loss.

**Your internet dropped (online test).**
Most platforms preserve your progress and allow re-entry. Reconnect immediately and, if there is a support contact, use it. Note the time of the disconnection in case you need to raise it later.

**You panicked and the numbers stopped making sense.**
Stop for fifteen seconds. Four counts in, six counts out, twice. Then answer the easiest question you can see. Arithmetic capability returns quickly once the acute response subsides; forcing through it does not work.

## A closing note

You have worked through a great deal of material to get here. Tomorrow's test does not measure that work directly — it measures a narrow, timed sample of it, under conditions designed to be uncomfortable.

Two things are worth holding onto:

**First**, the aptitude test is a filter, not a verdict. It screens for a specific kind of speed under a specific kind of pressure. It is not a measure of whether you would be good at the job, and everyone who has sat one knows it.

**Second**, you are better prepared than the median candidate simply by virtue of having a plan for how to spend the 42 minutes. Most people walk in without one. Trust the framework: read the units, watch the base, draw the diagram, answer everything, and move when the clock says move.

Good luck.
