# DETAILED SOLUTIONS

## Section 1 — Quantitative Aptitude

**Q1 → (c) ₹5,000.** Three-year difference formula:
$$P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2\left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) = P(0.01)(3.1) = 0.031P = 155 \implies P = ₹5{,}000$$

> Using the **two-year** formula P(R/100)² would give ₹15,500 — the standard trap.

**Q2 → (b) ₹150.** Equal SP with ±20% ⇒ loss of 20²/100 = **4%**.
$$CP_1 = \frac{1800}{1.2} = 1{,}500, \qquad CP_2 = \frac{1800}{0.8} = 2{,}250$$
$$\text{Total CP} = 3{,}750, \quad \text{Total SP} = 3{,}600 \implies \text{Loss} = ₹150$$

**Q3 → (c) 30 days.** Work = LCM(12, 15, 20) = 60 units.
$$A+B = 5, \quad B+C = 4, \quad A+C = 3$$
$$2(A+B+C) = 12 \implies A+B+C = 6$$
$$A = 6 - 4 = 2 \text{ units/day} \implies \frac{60}{2} = 30 \text{ days}$$

**Q4 → (c) 100 min.** Speed × 5/6 ⇒ time × 6/5.
$$\text{Extra time} = \frac{T}{5} = 20 \implies T = 100 \text{ minutes}$$

**Q5 → (c) 48 L.**

*Stage 1:* 60 L milk; add water for 3 : 2 ⇒ water = 40 L, total **100 L**.
*Stage 2:* remove 20 L of mixture ⇒ removes 60% of 20 = **12 L of milk**.
$$\text{Milk remaining} = 60 - 12 = 48 \text{ L}$$
Adding 20 L of water does not change the milk.

**Q6 → (c) 66.** Overlap formula:
$$6(58) + 6(63) - 11(60) = 348 + 378 - 660 = 66$$

**Q7 → (d) 49.**
$$\left\lfloor\frac{200}{5}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\frac{200}{25}\right\rfloor + \left\lfloor\frac{200}{125}\right\rfloor = 40 + 8 + 1 = 49$$

> Stopping after the first two terms gives 48 — the 125 term is easy to miss.

**Q8 → (b) 30 years.** 32 = 2⁵ ⇒ five doubling periods:
$$5 \times 6 = 30 \text{ years}$$

**Q9 → (c) 43,200.** Gap method:
$$5! \times \;^6P_4 = 120 \times 360 = 43{,}200$$
*(Five boys create six gaps; four girls occupy four of them.)*

**Q10 → (c) 24.**
$$V_{\text{cone}} = \tfrac13 \pi (81)(32) = 864\pi, \qquad V_{\text{sphere}} = \tfrac43 \pi (27) = 36\pi$$
$$\frac{864\pi}{36\pi} = 24$$

**Q11 → (c) ₹17,500.**
$$5x - 3y = 4000 \qquad 4x - 2y = 5000$$
From the second: 2x − y = 2500 ⇒ y = 2x − 2500.
$$5x - 3(2x - 2500) = 4000 \implies -x + 7500 = 4000 \implies x = 3500$$
$$\text{A's income} = 5(3500) = ₹17{,}500$$

*(Check: incomes 17,500 and 14,000; expenditures 13,500 and 9,000; savings 4,000 and 5,000 ✓)*

**Q12 → (b) 5/12.** Prime sums are 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11.

| Sum | 2 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 11 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ways | 1 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 2 | **15** |

$$P = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}$$

**Q13 → (b) 2 km/h.**
$$\text{Down} = \frac{24}{3} = 8, \quad \text{Up} = \frac{24}{6} = 4 \implies \text{Stream} = \frac{8-4}{2} = 2 \text{ km/h}$$

**Q14 → (a) 1.** Remainder cycle of 5 modulo 13:
$$5^1 \equiv 5, \quad 5^2 \equiv 12, \quad 5^3 \equiv 8, \quad 5^4 \equiv 1$$
Cycle length 4; 100 mod 4 = 0 ⇒ 5¹⁰⁰ ≡ 5⁴ ≡ **1**.

**Q15 → (b) 1,00,000.**
$$1.10 \times 0.90 \times 1.20 = 1.188$$
$$P = \frac{1{,}18{,}800}{1.188} = 1{,}00{,}000$$

**Q16 → (b) 20 L.** Milk = 70 L (unchanged), water = 10 L.
For 7 : 3 with milk at 70:
$$\text{Water required} = 70 \times \frac{3}{7} = 30 \implies \text{add } 30 - 10 = 20 \text{ L}$$

**Q17 → (b) 15.2%.**
$$1.60 \times 0.80 \times 0.90 = 1.152 \implies 15.2\% \text{ profit}$$

**Q18 → (c) 62.**
$$S_{10} = 3(100) + 50 = 350, \qquad S_9 = 3(81) + 45 = 288$$
$$a_{10} = 350 - 288 = 62$$

**Q19 → (b) 40 km/h.**
$$T = \frac{60}{30} + \frac{60}{40} + \frac{60}{60} = 2 + 1.5 + 1 = 4.5 \text{ h}$$
$$\text{Average} = \frac{180}{4.5} = 40 \text{ km/h}$$

**Q20 → (c) 1,683.** LCM(5, 6, 7, 8) = 840, so N = 840k + 3.
For divisibility by 9: 840 ≡ 3 (mod 9), so 3k + 3 ≡ 0 (mod 9) ⇒ k + 1 ≡ 0 (mod 3) ⇒ **k = 2**.
$$N = 840(2) + 3 = 1{,}683$$

## Section 3 — Verbal Ability

**Q36 → (b) Perceptive.** PERSPICACIOUS = having keen insight or discernment.
*(Root: per- = through, spic- = to see — "seeing through".)*

**Q37 → (b) Enduring.** EPHEMERAL = lasting a very short time. Options (a) Brief, (c) Fleeting and (d) Transient are all **synonyms**.

**Q38 → (c).** With **not only … but also**, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. The nearer subject is "directors" (plural) ⇒ **were present**, not "was present".

**Q39 → (a) Too weak.** The structure is **too … to**: *"too weak to support"* means it was insufficient. The alternatives require different completions — *"so weak that…"*, *"weak enough to…"* — which the sentence does not provide.

**Q40 → (b).** Two rules must both hold:
1. **Inversion** after an opening *Hardly*: "Hardly **had the meeting**…" ✓
2. **Correlative pairing**: *Hardly … **when*** ✓ (*than* pairs with *no sooner*)
3. Past participle **begun**, not "began" ✓

Only (b) satisfies all three.

**Q41 → (a) Pluralism.** In this sense, holding more than one office or position simultaneously.

**Q42 → (b) A victory won at so great a cost that it is scarcely worth winning.**
*(From Pyrrhus of Epirus, whose costly victories over Rome depleted his forces beyond recovery.)*

**Q43 → (a) RQSP.**
```
R  →  the change was made (topic sentence, no back-reference)
Q  →  what the change involved (checklist rather than open guidance)
S  →  how it was reinforced (monthly audit of compliance)
P  →  the outcome ("The result was…")
```
P must come last — "The result" refers back to everything preceding it.

**Q44 → (b) Underlying operations did not drive the margin improvement.**

The statement says the improvement was attributable **entirely** to a one-off disposal. That logically excludes any contribution from underlying operations.

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| (a) | Contradicted by "entirely" ✗ |
| **(b)** | **Follows necessarily** ✅ |
| (c) | Margins **improved** ✗ |
| (d) | A disposal is explicitly mentioned ✗ |

**Q45 → (b) Dwindle.** PROLIFERATE = to increase rapidly in number. Options (a), (c) and (d) are all synonyms or near-synonyms.

## Section 4 — Situational Judgement

These five are harder than the earlier sets because in each case **two options have merit**. The distinguishing factor is always the same: *which response addresses the substance while following the right process?*

---

**Q46 → (b).**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| (a) Remove as instructed | ❌ If the limitation is material, removing it makes the report misleading. "The manager owns the report" is not a defence. |
| **(b) Ask for the reasoning; escalate if still concerned** | **✅ Respects the manager, seeks their view first, and preserves the escalation route** |
| (c) Refuse and escalate immediately | ⚠️ The right instinct, wrong sequence. Going to the partner before hearing the manager's reasoning skips a rung — and the manager may have a good answer |
| (d) Remove but keep a copy | ❌ Protects you personally; the client still receives a misleading report |

> **Why (b) beats (c):** the manager may know something you do not — the limitation may be disclosed elsewhere, or genuinely immaterial. Option (b) finds out first **and** commits to escalating if the concern survives. Option (c) assumes bad faith.

---

**Q47 → (b).**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| (a) Self-review is better than none | ❌ Self-review provides no independent assurance — that is its defining limitation, not a minor one |
| **(b) Explain the limitation; ask what alternative can be arranged** | **✅ States the principle and seeks a workable solution** |
| (c) Do it and note the absence of independent review | ⚠️ Honest documentation, but proceeds with an inadequate process where an alternative might exist |
| (d) Sign without reviewing | ❌ |

> **Option (c) is the strongest distractor** because it is transparent. But transparency about an inadequate control is not the same as fixing it. Option (b) raises the issue *before* the deadline, when something can still be arranged.

---

**Q48 → (b).**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| (a) Respect the confidence | ❌ A confidence cannot cover falsified engagement data |
| **(b) Tell them they must disclose it; make clear you will if they do not** | **✅ Gives them the opportunity to self-report while making the outcome certain** |
| (c) Report immediately without speaking to them | ⚠️ Defensible, but forecloses self-disclosure, which is materially better for everyone including the file |
| (d) Advise them to correct it quietly | ❌ Compounds concealment |

> **This is the hardest scenario in the set.** Both (b) and (c) end with the matter being reported. Option (b) is preferred because self-disclosure is a better outcome — for the accuracy of the record, for the colleague, and for the firm's ability to assess the issue properly. The clause *"if you do not, I will have to"* is what makes it firm rather than merely hopeful.

---

**Q49 → (b).**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| (a) Perform it now, leave the record | ❌ The record would still misstate when the work was done |
| **(b) Inform the manager that the record is wrong, and perform the procedure** | **✅ Corrects both the work and the record** |
| (c) Amend the record silently | ❌ Unilateral change to a file record; the procedure remains unperformed |
| (d) Leave it — the conclusion is unchanged | ❌ A file that records unperformed work as complete is a documentation failure regardless of the conclusion |

> **The two-part structure is essential.** The *work* gap and the *record* gap are separate problems. Options (a) and (c) each fix one and leave the other.

---

**Q50 → (b).**

| Option | Assessment |
|---|---|
| (a) Accept and resign immediately | ❌ Abandons the engagement; creates an independence issue retrospectively |
| **(b) Decline substantive discussion until the engagement ends; disclose to your manager** | **✅ Removes the independence threat and is transparent about it** |
| (c) Continue privately | ❌ An undisclosed employment discussion with a client creates a live independence threat |
| (d) Use it to negotiate a raise | ❌ Uses a conflict of interest as leverage; still leaves it undisclosed |

> **Why disclosure matters even if you intend to decline.** An employment approach from an audit or advisory client is an independence matter with defined firm procedures. The threat exists from the moment the offer is made, not from the moment you consider accepting it.
